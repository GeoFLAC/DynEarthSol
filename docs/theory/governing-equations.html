Governing equations

With its default moving mesh, DynEarthSol follows the deforming material: nodes move with the velocity, so every quantity below is carried by the material and time derivatives follow it. This page collects the equations and the conventions; each section links to the page that treats it in detail.

Unknowns and conventions​

QuantityWhere it livesSymbol
velocity, positionnodesu, x
temperaturenodesT
pore pressure (with fluid flow)nodesp
stress, strain, plastic strainelementsσ, ε, εp​
material identitymarkers

Stress and strain are positive in tension; pore pressure is positive in compression. Depth is negative z (the top of the box is at z=0). In 2D the coordinates are x and z. Elements are linear simplices (triangles in 2D, tetrahedra in 3D), so strain and stress are constant in each element.

Momentum​

ρI​dtdu​=∇⋅σ+ρb​g.

DES integrates this equation explicitly. For slow tectonic deformation the inertia is scaled up (mass scaling) and damped (dynamic relaxation) to approach a quasistatic response, or each physical step is solved to equilibrium by pseudo-transient iteration. Scaled explicit/GVS runs still require force-balance and physical-step checks for their loading path. See Solution scheme.

ρI​ constructs the inertial mass, while ρb​ supplies physical weight. Mass scaling changes the former, not the gravitational load.

Constitutive law​

The stress follows the strain history through the material's rheology: elastic, viscous, Maxwell, elasto-plastic or elasto-visco-plastic, with optional rate-and-state friction. The stress rate is objective: after the constitutive update of each step, the stress and strain are rotated by the element's spin (the Jaumann rate). See Rheology for the laws, Constitutive update for their integration and Rate-and-state friction.

Density​

Without hydraulics, the solid density of an element varies with temperature, ρ=ρ0​[1−α(T−273 K)], averaged over the materials of its markers; it does not change with pressure or volumetric strain. It enters gravity, while volume change is resisted by the bulk modulus in the constitutive law. Hydraulics adds mixture weight, with saturation-dependent fluid content; the optional finite-density mode has inventory-based weight. These physical weights are distinct from the fictitious inertial mass used by quasistatic explicit/GVS mechanics. See Poromechanics.

Energy​

ρcp​dtdT​=∇⋅(k∇T)+ρH+Qs​+Qi​,

with radiogenic, shear and injected heat. See Heat transport.

Pore fluid​

With control.has_hydraulic_diffusion = yes, the stress in the momentum balance is the total stress of a fluid-saturated or partly saturated solid, σ=σ′−αB​χpI for effective stress σ′ and Biot coefficient αB​. χ=1 for saturated material and χ=Se​ for the Bishop retention law. The pore pressure obeys a fluid mass balance with Darcy flow. See Poromechanics.

Material and surface changes​

Markers carry material identity and change it by phase changes; the top surface erodes and receives sediment by surface processes; and the mesh is rebuilt when it distorts, with fields mapped to the new mesh (Remeshing).

Initial and boundary conditions​

Velocity, traction, temperature and pressure conditions and the initial temperature, stress and weak zones are set per model; see the User guide.
