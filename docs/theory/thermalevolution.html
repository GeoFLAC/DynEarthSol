Heat transport

Temperature controls the viscosity of rock, its density and, with pore fluid, its pore pressure. DES solves the heat equation on the same Lagrangian mesh as the mechanics:

ρcp​dtdT​=∇⋅(k∇T)+ρH+Qs​+Qi​,

where T is temperature, ρ density, cp​ heat capacity (mat.heat_capacity), k thermal conductivity (mat.therm_cond), H radiogenic heat production per unit mass, Qs​ shear heating and Qi​ heat injected at points or with fluid. Because the mesh moves with the material, dT/dt is the rate following the material: heat is carried by the deformation without a separate advection term. When the mesh is remeshed, nodal temperature is interpolated to the new nodes; see Remeshing.

Discretization​

Multiplying by a weighting function and integrating by parts over the domain gives, for node a,

Ca​T˙a​=−e∋a∑​(ke​Ωe​b∈e∑​Dab​Tb​)+e∋a∑​Mρe​He​Ωe​​+…,Dab​=i∑​∂xi​∂Na​​∂xi​∂Nb​​,

with M the number of nodes per element, the element volume Ωe​ and the linear shape functions Na​, whose gradients are constant in each simplex. The thermal mass is lumped:

Ca​=e∋a∑​Mρe​cp,e​Ωe​​.

The temperature is updated explicitly, Ta​←Ta​+ΔtT˙a​. The explicit update is stable only below a diffusion time step of order ρcp​h2/k. When the physical time step is longer, DES uses stable thermal substeps and applies sources over their actual intervals. Transport substeps do not make an oversized explicit mechanical step stable.

Boundary conditions​

Each side of the box is either insulated or held at a fixed temperature, set by bc.tbc_x0 … bc.tbc_z1 (0 insulated, 1 fixed) with the value bc.tbc_val_*. By default only the top is fixed, at bc.surface_temperature. Explicit thermal boundary settings apply from initialization; with only the legacy defaults, the initial profile is kept until the first transport update applies the top value. See Temperature for the initial profiles. There is no prescribed boundary heat flux; heat can be added through the sources below.

Heat sources​

Radiogenic heat. For the continental initial profiles (ic.temperature_option = 2 or 3), the heat production of each layer, mat.radiogenic_heat_prod (W/kg) of the material named in ic.radiogenic_heat_mat_in_layer, is stored in each element. It stays with the element, is remapped on remeshing and heats the model throughout the run. The field is written as radiogenic source.

Shear heating. With control.has_shear_heating = yes, the constitutive model's accepted nonelastic work becomes pending heat with unit efficiency. Rejected PT trials and the initial instantaneous equilibration do not deposit it. The pending quantity is extensive energy: changing the next physical timestep changes its rate of deposition, not the energy already accepted. Checkpoints and remapping preserve it. Reversible elastic, thermal and pore-pressure increments are excluded; see the plastic-heating reference.

Injected heat. [heat_injection] adds heat at points on a schedule, and [injection] fluid injected at a prescribed temperature carries its enthalpy into the model; see Poromechanics.

Groundwater heat advection​

With pore-fluid flow and control.has_fluid_thermal_advection = yes, Darcy flow also carries heat, and the heat capacity becomes that of the rock–water mixture, C=(1−ϕ)ρs​cs​+ϕρf​cf​. The advective term is added to the same explicit update as a Galerkin operator with added graph diffusion, which limits the advective contribution. With no flow it reduces to ordinary Galerkin conduction; on an arbitrary obtuse mesh it does not guarantee a maximum principle for the combined operator. Thermal substeps respect the advective limit. Buoyancy from fluid thermal expansion (mat.fluid_alpha) couples the flow to temperature. The formulation and its checks are in Poromechanics and the heat and groundwater benchmarks.

The capacity above belongs to the default hydraulic model. With evolving fluid storage it is not a complete retained-enthalpy ledger. The optional finite-density model tracks actual water mass, retained sensible energy and inlet/outlet heat; full pressure work remains outside it.

Coupling back to mechanics​

Density. The density of each material falls with temperature,

ρ=ρ0​[1−α(T−273 K)],

with the volumetric thermal expansion α (mat.alpha, default 3×10−5 K⁻¹). This drives thermal buoyancy and also enters the thermal mass, so with α=0 the diffusivity k/(ρcp​) varies slightly with temperature; the half-space cooling benchmark shows the effect.

Viscosity. Creep viscosity depends exponentially on temperature; see Rheology.

Thermal stress. With control.has_thermal_pressurization = yes, heating adds −KαΔT to the normal stresses and, through fluid/solid expansion, changes pore pressure. Hydraulic and thermal diffusion must both be enabled; enabling pore-fluid flow alone does not enable this stress term. With thermal pressurization disabled, solid thermal expansion still affects density.
