Markers and phase changes

The mesh moves with the material, but it is rebuilt when it distorts, and an element then no longer holds one rock. DES therefore tracks material identity with markers: points that move with the deformation, each carrying a material index. An element's properties are the marker-weighted means described in Rheology.

Markers​

Each marker stores the element that contains it and its barycentric coordinates there, so it moves exactly with the element and needs no search while the mesh only deforms. After remeshing, every marker is located in the new mesh.

Initial markers. markers.init_marker_option = 1 scatters markers.markers_per_element markers randomly in each element (seeded by markers.random_seed); 2 places them on a regular grid with spacing markers.init_marker_spacing, in units of mesh.resolution. Their material comes from ic.mattype_option: 0 takes the region of a .poly mesh, 1 assigns horizontal layers at ic.mattype_layer_depths (in units of mesh.zlength), and 101 calls a custom routine in the source.

Replenishment. Remeshing and stretching can leave an element with few markers. When it holds fewer than markers.min_num_markers_in_element, a marker is added. Its material follows markers.replenishment_option: 0 material 0, 1 drawn from the materials of the element and its neighbours in proportion, 2 the material of the nearest marker; the parameter reference lists the other options.

Phase changes​

A phase change converts a marker from one material to another when its pressure and temperature cross a reaction boundary; the element properties then follow the new marker counts. mat.phase_change_option selects the rules:

OptionRules
0none (default)
1a fixed set of subduction rules on fixed material indices 0–7
3the subduction rule engine: rules switched on one by one, materials named by parameters
101a custom routine in phasechanges.cxx

Phase changes need at least two materials.

Option 3: the subduction rule engine​

Each rule converts the material named by one mat.mattype_* parameter into another when its predicate holds. Depth d is measured below the current local free surface; temperature T is in °C below. The pressure P (compression positive) is the mean stress of the marker's element with mat.phase_change_pressure_option = 0, so tectonic over- and underpressure move the boundaries, or the lithostatic reference pressure at that depth with 1.

The antigorite field is the wedge between the lines through (730 °C, 2.1 GPa)–(500 °C, 7.5 GPa) and (730 °C, 2.1 GPa)–(650 °C, 0.2 GPa). Using one field for both serpentinite rules means no pressure and temperature satisfy both, so a marker cannot flip back and forth.

Where the mantle serpentinizes. Water released by the slab hydrates the mantle above it. DES finds the slab with a background grid of cells of size mesh.vt_size (in units of mesh.resolution): a cell is wet crust when more than mat.wet_crust_fraction_threshold of its markers are oceanic crust, sediment or sedimentary rock. A mantle marker serpentinizes when such a cell lies below it within mat.serpentinization_thickness, optionally only if the marker is also horizontally within the wet crust's extent plus mat.serpentinization_wet_crust_margin.

Each active rule needs its source and target materials set (mat.mattype_oceanic_crust, mat.mattype_eclogite, mat.mattype_serpentinite, mat.mattype_mantle, mat.mattype_hydrous_mantle, mat.mattype_sediment, mat.mattype_sediment_rock, mat.mattype_metasediment, mat.mattype_schist); DES stops at start-up if one is missing, out of range or equal to its target. The rules and boundaries follow those of GeoFLAC. The subduction tutorial uses this option.

Option 1: simple subduction rules​

Option 1 uses fixed material indices: 0 mantle, 1 serpentinized mantle, 2 oceanic crust, 3 eclogite, 4 sediment, 5 schist, 6 and 7 upper and lower continental crust, and the pressure is always the lithostatic reference pressure. Oceanic crust becomes eclogite above 500 °C and P>−0.3+0.0022TK​ GPa, with TK​ in kelvin; sediment becomes schist above 650 °C deeper than 20 km; serpentinite returns to mantle above 550 °C past the (730 °C, 2.1 GPa)–(500 °C, 7.5 GPa) line. With control.has_hydration_processes = yes, crust and sediment hotter than 150 °C release water as separate hydrous markers, which rise at control.hydration_migration_speed; mantle below 550 °C that holds a hydrous marker becomes serpentinite.
