Hantush–Jacob pumping from a leaky aquifer

A well pumps a confined aquifer covered by a leaky aquitard. Water drawn down through the aquitard limits the drawdown, which approaches a steady cone.

Largest drawdown error: 0.410% of CQ​, over 10≤r≤30 m and eight times through pumping and recovery. Leakage changes the drawdown by up to 53% of CQ​ from Theis.

At a glance
Dimension3D
Buildmake ndims=3 from the poromechanics source (not yet merged; DynEarthSol #134)
Inputshantush.zip
Reproduce withDynEarthSol 3774df0

Problem setup​

Geometry and well. A quarter box, 0≤x,y≤75 m. An aquifer B=2.5 m thick lies under an aquitard one element thick (b′=h) whose top is held at p=0. A well on the corner edge, screened through the aquifer only, pumps Q/4 (Q=10−4 m3/s) for 200 s and then shuts in.

Layers. The aquitard is a second material whose vertical leakance kz′​/(μb′) is the same on every mesh, so λ=15 m throughout; it stores a thousandth of the aquifer water per volume. Vertical permeability is ten times horizontal in both layers, so the aquifer has negligible vertical resistance, as the theory assumes.

What is compared. The pressure drawdown at the aquifer nodes with 10≤r≤30 m, at eight times through pumping and recovery, against the superposed Hantush–Jacob solution. The skeleton is stiff and pressure feedback is off.

Input and how to run​

Every file this example needs is in hantush.zip. Run from that folder:

# Runs the 3D quarter box with the one-element aquitard on top: 160 steps of 2 s, pumping for 200 s, then recovery to 320 s. DES writes hantush.save.* frames every 20 steps (40 s). On one thread this mesh takes about 45 minutes.
dynearthsol3d input.cfg

Result: DES and the analytical solution​

(a) Drawdown s_p/C_Q along the aquifer base on the y = 0 plane at four times during pumping: DES nodes (circles) and the Hantush–Jacob solution (lines). The dashed grey curve is the Theis solution without leakage at the last of these times, and the band marks the compared range 10–30 m. (b) Drawdown history at r ≈ 10 m and r ≈ 20 m: Hantush–Jacob (solid), Theis without leakage (dashed) and DES (circles); the dotted line marks shut-in. (c) Maximum and RMS drawdown error against mesh spacing, with a slope-2 guide.

This checks a layered aquifer system: an aquitard of a second material leaking from a fixed-pressure top, with the well, storage and radial diffusion of the Theis case. On the finest mesh the aquitard stores 0.03% of the aquifer's water, close to the no-storage assumption. Mechanical pressure feedback is disabled in this flow-isolation test.

To reproduce the whole study behind this figure, from the DES source folder:

python3 benchmarks/poromechanics/hantush_reference.py
python3 tests/functional/hydromechanics/check_hantush.py dynearthsol3d OUT --spacings 2.5 1.25 0.625

Analytical reference​

Tp​=κB/μ,Sp​=BSB​,λ2=κz′​κBb′​ Sp​∂t​sp​=Tp​(∂r2​sp​+r1​∂r​sp​)−λ2Tp​​sp​ sp​(r,t)=4πTp​Q​W(4Tp​tr2Sp​​,λr​) W(u,β)=∫u∞​y1​exp(−y−4yβ2​)dy W(u,0)=E1​(u) W(0,β)=2K0​(β)

sp​=pinitial​−p is the pressure drawdown, κ and κz′​ the horizontal aquifer and vertical aquitard permeabilities, and SB​ the fixed-stress storage of the Theis page. Shut-in is superposed as in Theis. Errors are fractions of CQ​=Q/(4πTp​).

Derivation​

With no aquitard storage the aquitard pressure is linear in z, so it leaks a flux (κz′​/μb′)sp​ into the aquifer. Integrating over the aquifer thickness adds this sink to the Theis equation.

qleak​=μb′κz′​​sp​=λ2Tp​​sp​

The Laplace transform in time and the line-sink limit at r→0 give the leaky well function W, which tends to Theis at early time and to the steady cone 2K0​(r/λ) at late time. hantush_reference.py checks both limits, the identity W(u,β)+W(β2/4u,β)=2K0​(β) and the equation itself.

Hantush and Jacob (1955), Non-steady radial flow in an infinite leaky aquifer, Trans. AGU 36, 95-100. Evaluated in hantush_reference.py by quadrature.

Original reference.

Validity and limits​

Hantush and Jacob neglect aquitard storage, horizontal aquitard flow and vertical resistance in the aquifer. Their dimensionless measures here are SB′​b′/Sp​≤0.12%, (b′/λ)2/10≤0.28% and B2/(20λ2)=0.14%, the first two falling with h. SB′​ is aquitard storage per volume and Sp​=BSB​ aquifer storage per area. Line well, no wellbore storage; the axis r<10 m is excluded.
