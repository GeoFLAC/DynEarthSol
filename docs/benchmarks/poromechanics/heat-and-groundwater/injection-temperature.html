Hot-water injection: Avdonin solution and heat ledger

Injected water carries its temperature. DES adds the injected enthalpy relative to the local temperature, which with fluid mass conservation makes its advective heat equation conserve energy. Two checks: radial hot-water injection against Avdonin’s solution, and a sealed doublet against an independent heat ledger.

Avdonin on the 0.1 m mesh: largest nodal error 1.50% of Tin​−T0​ at 5.8 d (rms 0.28%). Doublet: stored heat follows the ledger within 0.019% after the first step, through breakthrough (producer at 78% of Tin​−T0​).

Problem setup​

Option. injection.temperature gives the temperature Tin​ of the fluid of each point source; it requires fluid thermal advection. Unset, the injected fluid carries no heat, and any heat source must be given separately.

(a) Avdonin. A line source injects q=2×10−5 m2/s at Tin​=333 K into a layer at T0​=283 K. DES models a quarter plane, 8 m square, with the source at its corner, sealed and insulated symmetry sides, drained far sides and a stiff skeleton without pressure feedback.

(b) Doublet. An 8 m sealed, insulated box with an injector at Tin​ and a producer 4 m away at the same rate q=10−4 m2/s. The hot front reaches the producer, so heat leaves at a changing temperature.

What is compared. (a) Nodal temperature against the similarity solution at four times; stored against injected heat. (b) Stored heat against the independent ledger of injected minus extracted heat at every step.

Input and how to run​

Every file this example needs is in injection-temperature.zip. Run from that folder:

# Avdonin case: injects hot water at the corner of the 8 m quarter plane (0.1 m mesh) for 500 steps of 1000 s (5.8 d), saving hot.save.* every 125 steps (four times) with hot.info listing the frames.
dynearthsol2d input.cfg
# Doublet case, run in its own directory because both inputs write files named hot.*: 400 steps of 1500 s in the sealed 8 m box, saving every step.
mkdir -p doublet && cp input-doublet.cfg doublet/ && (cd doublet && dynearthsol2d input-doublet.cfg)

Analytical reference​

CTt​+ρf​cf​q⋅∇T=∇⋅(λ∇T)+ρf​cf​Q(Tin​−T) Tin​−T0​T−T0​​=Γ(ν)Γ(ν,u)​ u=4λtr2C​,ν=4πλρf​cf​q​ E(t)−E(0)=ρf​cf​q∫0t​(Tin​−Tprod​)ds

C is the bulk heat capacity, Q the injected volume rate per unit volume, Γ(ν,u) the upper incomplete gamma function and E=∑n​CAn​Tn​ the stored sensible heat. Tprod​ is the temperature at the producer, interpolated with the element's shape functions.

Derivation​

The conservative balance ∂t​(CT)+∇⋅(ρf​cf​qT)=∇⋅(λ∇T)+ρf​cf​QTin​ gives the advective form above once fluid mass conservation, ∇⋅q=Q, removes ρf​cf​T∇⋅q. Extraction removes fluid at the local temperature, which the advective term already carries, so extracting points add nothing.

For radial flow q=qr^/(2πr) the equation has a similarity solution in u; the injected heat ρf​cf​q(Tin​−T0​)t is all stored, since none leaves. avdonin_reference.py checks both.

Avdonin’s radial thermal-injection problem (1964). The similarity solution is derived here and checked against the equation and the heat balance in avdonin_reference.py; the ledger follows from energy conservation alone.

Validity and limits​

Fixed heat capacity: heat carried by fluid that goes into storage is not tracked, so a sealed cell storing all its injection is outside the scope. No pressure work, dissipation or thermal expansion. Thermal advection uses the Darcy flux at the start of each step, which loses part of the first step of heat when a source starts from rest and that step needs several thermal substeps.
