Plastic tunnel: Salençon solution

A circular tunnel is excavated in rock under uniform in-situ stress: the support pressure on its wall is lowered to zero, and a ring of rock around the opening yields. Salençon’s solution gives the stresses, the plastic radius and the wall convergence for a perfectly plastic Mohr–Coulomb material.

On the 0.05 m mesh the hoop stress is within 1.57% of P (RMS), the displacement within 0.21% of the wall convergence, and the plastic radius within 0.34%. The radial strain error, 3.88% of P/G, is concentrated within half a radius of the wall.

Problem setup​

Geometry and load. A quarter annulus with opening radius a=1 m and outer radius R=20a, in plane strain. The rock starts under uniform compression P=500 kPa in all three directions. The support pressure on the opening wall is lowered from P to zero in 16 equal steps, while the outer boundary keeps the pressure P.

Constraints. The two straight symmetry cuts have zero normal displacement. Gravity, fluid flow and heat are off, and the mesh does not move.

Material. Perfectly plastic Mohr–Coulomb: c=100 kPa, ϕ=30∘, associated flow (ψ=30∘), G=10 GPa, K=21.7 GPa (ν=0.3). No softening and no weak zone.

What is compared. Radial, hoop and out-of-plane stress, radial and hoop strain, and radial displacement for 1.05a≤r≤5a after the support is removed, on meshes refined near the opening to 0.2, 0.1 and 0.05 m, against the finite-annulus Salençon solution.

Input and how to run​

Every file this example needs is in salencon.zip. Run from that folder:

# Meshes annulus.poly (0.05 m near the opening, coarser outward) and writes the initial state: uniform 500 kPa compression, the wall still supported at 500 kPa, frame 0 with a checkpoint.
dynearthsol2d seed.cfg
# Fifteen unloading steps, load-001.cfg to load-015.cfg in order. Each restarts from the previous one and lowers the wall support pressure by P/16 (load-path.json lists them); one PT step per file brings the rock to equilibrium.
ls load-0*.cfg | xargs -n 1 dynearthsol2d
# The last step removes the support completely; this is the state compared with Salençon. input.cfg is the same file, and the shipped load-015 frame lets you run it without the earlier steps (but the displacement in the figure sums the velocities of every step).
dynearthsol2d salencon.cfg

Analytical reference​

N=1−sinϕ1+sinϕ​ q=2cN​ sr​=CrN−1−N−1q​,sθ​=Nsr​+q(a≤r≤b) C=aN−1Pi​+q/(N−1)​ sr​=A−B/r2,sθ​=A+B/r2(b≤r≤R) CbN−1−N−1q​=(N+1)R2−(N−1)b2(2P−q)R2+qb2​

sr​ and sθ​ are radial and hoop stress, compression positive (DES output is tension positive). Pi​ is the support pressure on the wall, here zero at the end, and b the plastic radius: 1.39575 m for R=20a (1.39405 m for an infinite domain). A and B follow from continuity of sr​ at b and sr​=P at R. The displacement integrates the plastic flow rule inside b and the elastic solution outside it; the derivation gives it.

Derivation​

Inside the plastic zone, radial equilibrium sr′​+(sr​−sθ​)/r=0 with the yield condition sθ​=Nsr​+q gives a power law in r; the wall pressure Pi​ fixes C. Outside it the Lamé solution holds. Requiring the elastic stress at b to reach yield gives the scalar equation for b, solved numerically.

sr′​+(sr​−sθ​)/r=0

For the displacement, the plastic strains follow the flow rule ϵrp​=−Mϵθp​ with M=(1+sinψ)/(1−sinψ), and the out-of-plane plastic strain is zero. Adding the elastic strains of plane-strain Hooke’s law gives u′+Mu/r as a known function of r; integrating from b, where u matches the elastic solution, gives u in the plastic zone. Strain and displacement are measured from the initially compressed state.

u′+rM​u=ere​+Meθe​

Salençon (1969), Contraction quasi-statique d’une cavité à symétrie sphérique ou cylindrique dans un milieu élastoplastique, Annales des Ponts et Chaussées 4, 231–236; the solution as given in the Itasca verification example for a cylindrical hole in a Mohr–Coulomb material.

Component check: return to a shear edge​

The tunnel comparison also needs a correct material-point return. This check uses tension-positive stress, unlike the compression-positive tunnel reference above. In the order (xx,zz,yy), define

C=λ11T+2GI,A=(N0​0N​−1−1​).

The rows of B replace the friction factor N by the dilation factor M. If the trial reaches both adjacent shear faces, the independent reference solves the two active constraints together:

(ACBT)η=Aσtr−q1, σ=σtr−CBTη.

The multipliers must be nonnegative and the returned principals ordered. The tensile cap must also hold. DES keeps the existing single-face return when its principals remain ordered; otherwise its shared shear routine checks both adjacent edges and restores the repeated eigenvalue.

For the same rock, an associated trial with sorted principals (169,185,200) kPa exceeds the tensile cap T=173.205 kPa, yet its admissible shear return is (165.347657,170.585939,170.585939) kPa. Both shear multipliers are positive and the tensile constraint is inactive. Therefore the trial maximum alone cannot select tensile clipping. Both 2D plane strain and 3D test the uncut shear return before accepting a tensile/composite branch. Rotated material-point tests and 1/16/64-increment paths check the stress and plastic history independently of stress smoothing. These controls qualify constant strength; they do not establish general apex, mixed tensile/shear or evolving-strength accuracy.

Why small force residuals do not establish convergence​

With no mixed-stress smoothing, the coarse tunnel develops alternating pressure between elements with an edge and a vertex on the wall. The out-of-plane stress remains the intermediate principal until the apex, so the shear-edge correction does not explain this spatial mode. Mixed stress suppresses the alternating pressure, but projects stress after the plastic return. The projected state can leave local yield and return again at the next step. This is a plausible explanation of the load-hold drift, rather than a demonstrated defect in the local return.

At fixed mesh, the 16/32/64-stage sequences do not demonstrate convergence in the load increment. At the matching 32 stages, refining 0.05 to 0.025 m reduces hoop error from 3.09% to 1.24% and displacement error from 0.372% to 0.113%, while radial-strain error remains 4.44% of P/G. The selected 16-stage result must be read with this limit. No correction of both spatial effects has been qualified.

Validity and limits​

Dry, isotropic loading, small strain, perfectly plastic. The radial strain misses a 3% target (3.88% of P/G on the finest mesh), almost all of it within half a radius of the wall. With mixed stress (needed here) the stored stress readjusts slightly at every step, so the result depends on the number of unloading steps: on the 0.05 m mesh, 16, 32 and 64 steps give hoop errors of 1.57%, 3.09% and 6.71%. Use the 16 steps shipped here, and do not hold the final load for many extra steps. Without mixed stress the coarse mesh diverges near the wall. Zero dilation is not covered.
