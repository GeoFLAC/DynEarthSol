Shear bands with and without rate-and-state friction

A brittle crust pulled apart from a weak seed localizes into shear bands. This benchmark runs the same model three times, with static Mohr–Coulomb friction (EP) and with rate-and-state friction that is velocity strengthening or velocity weakening (EP-RSF). It asks two questions: does rate-and-state friction change where and at what angle the bands form, and does it change how they slip? The inputs are the DES v2 benchmark in benchmarks/geodynamics/localization/; see rate-and-state friction for the friction law and for global velocity scaling.

Buildmake ndims=2

Setup​

SettingValue
Domain20 km × 10 km, uniform triangles of 100 m (meshing_option = 1)
Loadingboth sides pulled outward at 0.5 cm/yr each; free top; Winkler support at the base; gravity 10 m/s²
Initial statelithostatic stress; a circular seed of radius 500 m centred at (10, −9.5) km starts with plastic strain 1
Elasticity and densityK = 50 GPa, G = 30 GPa, 2700 kg/m³
Strengthfriction angle 30°, no dilation; cohesion softening from 30 to 0.1 MPa over plastic strain 0 to 0.5
Rate-and-stateaging law, Dc​ = 5 mm, V0​=10−9 m/s, strain-rate slip measure (rsf_slip_rate_projection_option = 1), state limit rsf_dtheta_max = 0.2
Mechanicsexplicit, global velocity scaling with c=2×105, damping factor 0.8
Duration12,000 years, a frame every 500 years

The three inputs differ only in the friction law:

InputRheologyaba/b
ep_phi30_psi0.cfgelasto-plastic–––
ep_rsf_strengthening_phi30_psi0.cfgelasto-plastic-rsf0.0180.0151.2
ep_rsf_weakening_phi30_psi0.cfgelasto-plastic-rsf0.0030.0150.2

For a Mohr–Coulomb material with friction angle ϕ and dilation angle ψ, the Arthur angle predicts bands at 45°+(ϕ+ψ)/4 from the direction of the least compressive stress, here 45°+30°/4=52.5° from the horizontal.

Input and how to run​

Build dynearthsol2d in the DES root, then run each input from the benchmark folder:

cd benchmarks/geodynamics/localization
mkdir -p output
OMP_NUM_THREADS=8 ../../../dynearthsol2d ep_phi30_psi0.cfg
OMP_NUM_THREADS=8 ../../../dynearthsol2d ep_rsf_strengthening_phi30_psi0.cfg
OMP_NUM_THREADS=8 ../../../dynearthsol2d ep_rsf_weakening_phi30_psi0.cfg

Each writes to output/ under its own name: frames <name>.save.*, checkpoints, <name>.info, and three monitor files, <name>_monitor_point_0.csv to _2.csv, recorded every 1000 steps. Point 1 starts at the seed centre; points 0 and 2 lie near the side walls.

What to look for​

Where the bands form​

All three models break the crust along a band that rises from the seed to the surface on the right, with a fainter conjugate band on the left. The band carries most of the extension; the rest of the crust stays elastic.

ModelBand dip (fit over 2–8 km)
EP52.4°
EP-RSF, strengthening54.5°
EP-RSF, weakening51.2°
Arthur angle, 45°+(ϕ+ψ)/452.5°

Rate-and-state friction moves the dip by about two degrees at most. The orientation is set by the Mohr–Coulomb strength, not by the rate dependence. Velocity strengthening steepens the band slightly; velocity weakening flattens it slightly.

The dip is measured from the plastic strain gained since frame 0, so the initial seed does not count. In each 200 m depth row between 2 and 8 km, take the element with the largest gain on each side of the seed, keep it if the gain exceeds a quarter of the maximum, and fit a straight line through the points:

import numpy as np
from Dynearthsol import Dynearthsol

des = Dynearthsol('output/ep_phi30_psi0')
first, last = des.frames[0], des.frames[-1]
x = des.read_field(last, 'coordinate')/1e3          # km
c = des.read_field(last, 'connectivity')
gain = des.read_field(last, 'plastic strain') - des.read_field(first, 'plastic strain')
cen = x[c].mean(axis=1)
zs, xs = [], []
for z0 in np.arange(-8.0, -2.0, 0.2):
    row = (cen[:, 0] >= 10) & (cen[:, 1] >= z0) & (cen[:, 1] < z0 + 0.2)
    i = np.argmax(np.where(row, gain, -np.inf))
    if gain[i] > 0.25*gain.max():
        zs.append(cen[i, 1]); xs.append(cen[i, 0])
slope = np.polyfit(zs, xs, 1)[0]
print('dip', np.degrees(np.arctan(1/abs(slope))))

Run it from the benchmark folder with the DES root on PYTHONPATH. It gives the dips in the table to within 0.1°. Use cen[:, 0] < 10 for the band on the left; in these runs only the right band passes the threshold over the whole depth range.

How the bands slip​

The monitor at the seed (_monitor_point_1.csv) shows three different ways of slipping on almost the same band. The stress invariant is sqrt(0.25*(stress_0 - stress_1)**2 + stress_2**2) of the seed element.

  • Loading is the same in all three. The stress rises linearly for about 4000 years while the crust deforms elastically.
  • EP fails once, then slides. The stress peaks at 86 MPa at 4.1 kyr. At 5.0 kyr the band breaks through to the surface in one fast slip: the seed moves about 12 m at up to 5 cm/s. After that the band slides steadily at about 78 MPa.
  • Velocity strengthening creeps. The stress peaks lower, at 79 MPa at 4.0 kyr, and the band then slides steadily at about 68 MPa. The seed never exceeds 1 cm/s and there are no slip events. The friction coefficient in the seed element (dynamic_friction) varies between about 0.54 and 0.81 from one monitor row to the next.
  • Velocity weakening sticks and slips. The stress reaches 85 MPa before the first event at 4.3 kyr. Then 39 events follow until 12 kyr. The gap between events shortens from about 440 years to about 180 years after 8 kyr. In a typical event the seed slips 1.3 m (0.5 to 2.0 m) in about a minute at 0.1 to 0.8 m/s, and the stress in the seed element drops by 5 MPa (up to 21 MPa). Most events are followed a few years later by a slow slip of about 0.3 m with no stress drop. Between events the stress recovers along a straight line, as in the loading phase.

The boundaries pull at the same rate in all three, and the seed has moved 68 to 77 m by 12 kyr in each. What differs is when that slip happens. Global velocity scaling lets one run carry both the centuries between events and the seconds of each event: the time step falls from about 106 s between events to below 10−3 s during them.

Limits​

  • One element. The stress and friction histories come from the seed element and the displacement from one node. They show the character of slip, not the slip distribution along the band.
  • One resolution. All runs use 100 m elements, so the band is a few elements wide. The dips have not been checked at other resolutions.
  • One band. Only the band on the right is clear enough to fit over 2–8 km. The conjugate band is weaker in all three models.

Exercises​

  • Change the dilation angle. Set dilation_angle0 and dilation_angle1 to 15 and compare the dips with the Arthur angle, now 56.25°.
  • Move a/b through 1. Run a = 0.012, 0.015 and 0.016 with b = 0.015 and find where the slip events stop.
  • Count the events. With sim.seismic_moment_calculate_output = yes the weakening case writes each event's moment to seismic_moment_magnitude.txt. Compare it with the seed slip in the monitor file.
