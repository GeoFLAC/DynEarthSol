Choosing a mechanical solver (PT)

DES3D offers two mechanical solution paths. The default, control.has_PT = no, advances the explicit momentum equation using the configured damping and mass scaling. With control.has_PT = yes, a pseudo-transient (PT) solve seeks mechanical equilibrium within each physical timestep.

PT is useful when each physical increment should satisfy a stated force balance tolerance. Its pseudo iterations are a numerical relaxation process; they do not resolve physical wave propagation. Assess a dynamic problem using the explicit momentum formulation and appropriate inertia, mass scaling and timestep choices.

This page covers solver selection and controls. The theory explains the method; the tutorials progress from elastic loading and equilibrium to Maxwell creep under a held load.

Physical steps and pseudo iterations​

A physical step has a physical duration, controlled by the usual timestep settings such as control.fixed_dt (seconds). Thermal diffusion, when enabled, advances temperature once before the PT solve; temperature then remains fixed during the mechanical trials. During the PT solve:

  1. The reference mesh and the physical-start material history are held fixed.
  2. Each candidate velocity is constrained by the velocity boundary conditions.
  3. The constitutive law evaluates the candidate over the same physical dt, starting again from the physical-start history.
  4. The solver accepts one candidate when its projected force residual meets the tolerance. Hydraulic transport, mesh motion and stress rotation then follow in their physical update stages.

Increasing the number of pseudo iterations does not repeatedly advance plastic strain, viscous relaxation or frictional aging. It also does not improve physical-time resolution; check the physical timestep separately. See PT mechanics for the equations and acceptance contract.

Enable PT and choose an iteration scheme​

These options belong in the input file's existing [control] section:

[control]
has_PT = yes
PT_option = 0
PT_max_iter = 5000
PT_relative_tolerance = 1e-6
PT_absolute_tolerance = 0
ParameterDefaultMeaning
has_PTnoUse a PT mechanical solve for each physical step.
PT_option00: localized dual-time stress/velocity relaxation; 1: adaptive dynamic relaxation (ADR).
PT_max_iter5000Maximum loop passes in one mechanical solve.
PT_CFL0.25Pseudo-time relaxation control for option 0. Unused by option 1.
PT_Re14.90188239869415Dual-time relaxation control for option 0. Unused by option 1.
PT_relative_tolerance1e-6Relative force tolerance in the default convergence mode.
PT_absolute_tolerance0Absolute force tolerance, in N/m in 2D or N in 3D.
PT_use_running_scalenoUse the alternative load-history scale for physical solves.
PT_stagnation_window500Warn after this many iterations without a new best residual; 0 disables the warning.

Both schemes use the same constitutive candidates, force residual, acceptance test and failure handling. Option 1 derives fictitious masses from an elastic/viscous reference stiffness and adapts its damping. Compare the options on a checked model; an iteration advantage in one example does not establish a general speedup. The legacy damping_factor is not a PT convergence control: artificial damping is excluded from the PT residual.

control.PT_jump has been removed. Delete it from older input files. The parameter reference is examples/defaults.cfg, also available through ./dynearthsol2d --help.

Understand convergence​

The solver assembles undamped nodal forces, including gravity, applied tractions and foundation loads. It projects these forces onto the velocity directions the boundary conditions leave free. Constrained support reactions therefore do not count as disequilibrium. The residual is the root mean square (RMS) force over the free rank; an entirely constrained model has zero free residual.

With the default PT_use_running_scale = no, acceptance requires

R≤PT_absolute_tolerance+PT_relative_toleranceRwarm​.

Rwarm​ is the first candidate's force imbalance, using the preceding physical velocity. It is not the gross prestress. If that trial does not converge, the solver may compare it with a candidate having zero free velocity and retain the smaller finite residual. That comparison does not change the relative-tolerance reference.

Absolute tolerance is a nodal force RMS, not a traction or stress in Pa. Its meaning depends on dimension and mesh. A relative-only target can become smaller than floating-point cancellation permits as the warm residual shrinks. Inspect the printed residual and threshold when diagnosing a failure near an already equilibrated state.

Optional running scale​

The elastic and Maxwell tutorials explicitly set:

[control]
PT_use_running_scale = yes

For physical steps this replaces the default criterion with

R≤max(10−6Rn​,1000ϵdouble​Fabs​).

Rn​ tracks the largest first warm residual of accepted physical solves, including the current candidate when eligible. Fabs​ is an absolute assembled element-force magnitude scale from the first trial, held fixed during that solve. The second term is a heuristic roundoff allowance, not a bound on stress or displacement error.

Initial equilibration still uses the absolute/relative criterion. Explicitly setting either tolerance with running scale enabled prints a warning because those settings no longer control physical-step convergence. Initial equilibration does not enter the running history. Remeshing retains the history but excludes the first successful post-remesh solve from increasing it.

An earlier large load can make this criterion too permissive for a later small perturbation. Its general use with history-dependent plastic, viscous or rate-and-state laws remains unvalidated beyond the documented examples. Use it with a known reference response and assess sensitivity to the loads, timestep and mesh. The solver default remains no.

Read a PT report​

With sim.info_display_step_interval = 1 and mesh.quality_check_step_interval = 1, every physical step prints a record:

PT step=2 status=converged iterations=1 residual=0.0342014 initial=0.0342014 threshold=0.0353553 warm_initial=35355.3 evaluations=2 stage=before_physical_updates

This shortened record comes from the 2D elastic tutorial, option 0.

FieldInterpretation
stepPhysical simulation step.
statusconverged, max_iterations or nonfinite.
iterationsPT loop passes. A value of 1 means no relaxation update was needed.
evaluationsFull constitutive/force trials, including candidate comparisons and restoration.
initialResidual of the selected starting candidate.
warm_initialFirst warm candidate's residual; the reference for default relative convergence.
residual, thresholdAccepted residual and its acceptance limit.
stageFor physical steps: after any thermal update, before hydraulic transport, mesh motion and stress rotation.

The accepted vector is saved as PT residual force, supported by Dynearthsol.py and 2vtk.py. Legacy force retains damping and support reactions. See PT force output before comparing these fields or recomputing force from saved stress.

Failure, restart and limits​

A stagnation warning does not stop the solve or relax the tolerance. Reaching PT_max_iter without convergence exits with 54; a nonfinite candidate exits with 50. Mechanical history is restored and the failed candidate does not proceed to output, hydraulic transport or mesh motion. This restores mechanical history; an earlier thermal update is not rolled back. Review the loading, constraints, physical timestep and attainable tolerance before changing the iteration limit.

Restart requires an explicitly selected checkpoint. With unchanged controls, PT restores the saved velocity, next-step dt and velocity scale without repeating completed initial equilibration. Running scale also requires its checkpointed history; old checkpoints without that history cannot restart with it enabled. Hydraulic checkpoints require the pending pressure increment consumed by the next mechanical step. Use one OpenMP thread for byte-exact continuation; multi-thread ADR reductions can differ in the last bits.

PT establishes equilibrium on the reference configuration. It does not guarantee equilibrium after mesh motion, convergence through strain softening, or accuracy for arbitrary hydraulic coupling. Start with the elastic tutorial, then the Maxwell creep tutorial. The solver notes and validation limits describe the available qualification in detail.
